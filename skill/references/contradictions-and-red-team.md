# Contradiction Management and Red-Team Review

## Identify the Conflict

Separate wording differences, scope differences, and genuine contradiction. Two sources may both be correct because they use different definitions, populations, or time periods.

For a material disagreement, examine these factors in order:

1. version or model;
2. date and period;
3. country, market, or jurisdiction;
4. population and inclusion criteria;
5. definition of the metric or outcome;
6. measurement or analysis method;
7. test or use conditions;
8. corrected data or later updates;
9. source quality, independence, and conflicts of interest.

When one source is stronger, connect the reason for preferring it to the type of claim. Do not treat reputation or official status alone as sufficient reason to override another source.

When conflict remains unresolved, report each credible conclusion, its scope, and the effect of the disagreement on the answer. Do not delete one side merely to create a cleaner narrative.

## Run the Red Team

After a provisional conclusion is formed and before final writing, examine these questions:

- What credible evidence contradicts the conclusion?
- Did the search or source selection suffer from confirmation bias?
- Was inconsistent evidence underweighted or omitted?
- Does the conclusion exceed the scope of the data?
- Was an association incorrectly interpreted as causal?
- Could a hidden assumption or user-context factor change the result?
- Is there a simpler or stronger alternative explanation?
- What evidence could change the conclusion?
- Under what conditions would the conclusion fail?

Do not use the Red Team for theatrical opposition. If the conclusion survives the test, state that no new material objection was found. If a consequential problem is found, correct the research, analysis, or conclusion scope before answering.

## Transfer to the Final Answer

Reflect the strongest unresolved conflict, decisive limitation, and condition that could change the conclusion in **Disagreements and Limitations**. Exclude repetitive detail and low-impact criticism.
