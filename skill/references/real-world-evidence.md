# Real-World Evidence

## Purpose

Use real-world evidence to discover user experience, practical problems, failure conditions, differences between claimed and observed performance, and possible patterns. Do not use it as a substitute for official evidence, scientific evidence, or controlled testing.

## Suitable Sources

- specialist communities and forums;
- user and owner reports;
- issue and error registries;
- long-term reviews;
- retail reviews, with substantial caution;
- observational data or real-world registries in the scientific sense.

Record the source type, assessable author identity, use conditions, and possible conflicts of interest.

## Detecting a Pattern

Before reporting a possible pattern, examine:

- recurrence across independent sources;
- similarity of occurrence conditions;
- alignment of model, version, date, and environment;
- technical evidence or an official response;
- consequence severity and reproducibility;
- contrary reports or users who did not experience the issue.

Do not convert a number of posts, reviews, or complaints into an occurrence rate without a valid denominator. Treat repeated reports only as a **signal of a possible pattern** unless representative data and a valid method exist.

## Biases

Consider self-selection bias, concentration of dissatisfied users, survivorship effects, commissioned reviews, coordinated activity, inauthentic accounts, and differences in user skill.

Present a single account only as a limited example, not a general conclusion. Do not republish unnecessary personal or sensitive details.

## Presentation

Keep real-world findings separate from official specifications, independent tests, and scientific evidence. Use wording such as “several similar reports were found” only with an explanation of sample limitations. Avoid terms such as “common” or “high probability” without valid data.
