# Source Strategy and Freshness

## Select Sources by Claim Type

| Claim type | Suitable starting source | Additional control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official product specification | Manufacturer documentation | Check version and market; use independent testing for real performance |
| Law or regulation | Official text or competent authority | Effective date, amendments, and jurisdiction |
| Scientific or medical finding | Original paper, credible review, or guideline | Study method, population, limitations, and freshness |
| Company financial report | Official report or filing | Reporting period, unit, audit status, and independent analysis when needed |
| Public statistic | Data-producing authority or original dataset | Metric definition, method, period, and revisions |
| Real product performance | Credible independent test | Match model, version, test conditions, and sample |
| User experience | Real-world evidence | Sample bias, occurrence conditions, and absence of a valid prevalence rate |
| Current news claim | Source close to the event and a credible news organization | Event time versus publication time and independent confirmation |

Do not treat this table as a fixed hierarchy. Select the best source according to the nature of the specific claim.

## Primary Sources

Reach the origin for decisive claims:

- official documents or statements;
- the text of a law or regulation;
- original papers or data;
- financial reports or regulatory filings;
- standards, patents, or technical documentation;
- manufacturer announcements and specifications.

A primary source establishes what the data owner or claimant said. It does not guarantee neutrality or external truth by itself.

## Independent Sources

For claims involving commercial, organizational, or promotional interests, find a suitable independent assessment. Examine financial independence, test method, data transparency, and reproducibility. Do not label an affiliated source as independent.

## Freshness

For changing information, record and check:

- publication date and event date;
- software version, product model, or document edition;
- country, market, or jurisdiction;
- statistical or financial period;
- later correction, update, or withdrawal.

Do not present historical information as current without a label. If the latest source is unavailable, state the most recent verifiable date and explain the effect of that limitation.

## Quality and Conflicts of Interest

Examine author or institutional credibility, expertise, method, visible data, conflicts of interest, primary versus republished status, and independent confirmation. Do not treat professional appearance, reputation, or search rank as sufficient evidence of credibility.

Use a weak source only when the question specifically requires that evidence type or no better source exists. Preserve its nature and limitations explicitly.
