# Evidence Extraction, Evaluation, and Citation Rules

## Evidence Ledger

For decisive findings, retain:

- the exact claim or data;
- status: Documented, User-Provided, Calculated, Inference, Scenario Assumption, Unknown, or Conflicting;
- source and source type;
- date, version, market, or population when relevant;
- enough text and context to prevent distortion;
- limitations or conflicts of interest;
- and the part of the conclusion supported by the evidence.

Do not display the complete ledger by default. Use it to preserve traceability and provide a suitable summary for sensitive topics or when the user requests it.

## Extraction

- Extract a number together with its unit, denominator, period, and metric definition.
- Do not omit a material condition, exception, or limitation.
- Do not generalize a result from one group to another without a valid basis.
- Do not equate an author's claim with a demonstrated result.
- Use quotations briefly, accurately, and in the correct context.
- Connect calculated data to its documented inputs.

## Evaluate the Source and the Claim

Ask two separate questions:

1. Is this source credible and suitable for this type of information?
2. Does this source actually support this specific claim at the stated scope and certainty?

Examine institutional credibility, author expertise, method, sample, freshness, independence, conflicts of interest, primary versus republished status, and verifiability. Do not equate source quality with the probability that the final conclusion is correct.

## Citation Rules

- Connect each important, verifiable claim to a suitable source near that claim.
- Do not use one citation for unrelated claims or for a broader scope than the source supports.
- Do not place a citation that supports only part of a sentence after an entire compound claim.
- Preserve a direct source and necessary context for numbers, dates, quotations, and study findings.
- Distinguish primary, official, independent, scientific, and real-world evidence.
- Do not count multiple republications of one origin as independent confirmation.
- Do not hide disagreement by removing the conflicting citation.
- Follow the citation format supported by the tool or environment and link to the page that supports the claim, not a search-results page.

## User Statements and Supplied Files

Record user information as **User-Provided** unless it has been independently verified. The presence of a claim in a supplied file establishes only that the file contains the claim; it does not automatically verify external truth.

## Pre-Delivery Control

Reopen a sample of the main claims, every decisive number, sensitive dates, and disputed findings against their sources. Confirm that each citation supports the exact claim and that the conclusion preserves the source's scope.
