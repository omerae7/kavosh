# Search Strategy and Deep Research

## Search Design

1. Convert the main question into independent, prioritized subquestions.
2. Determine the desired answer type and source type for each subquestion.
3. Specify the exact product, version, organization, law, study, market, and time period when relevant.
4. Extract synonyms, former names, abbreviations, alternative spellings, and specialist terminology.
5. Connect each search query to a target claim and source type. Do not perform unfocused general searching.

## Search Patterns

- direct search for an answer or specification;
- domain-restricted search of an official or scientific authority;
- search for the original document, dataset, or paper;
- search for a contrary claim or limitation;
- search for a specific version, date, country, or market;
- search for errors, problems, long-term experience, or failure reports;
- follow a secondary source's references back to the primary origin.

Do not treat one unsuccessful search as absence of information. Revise the query, terminology, language, time range, or source type. Report absence of evidence only after a reasonable and proportionate search.

## Deep Research Mode

Run deep research in multiple passes:

### Pass 1 — Topic Map

Identify terminology, actors, reference sources, key claims, and initial gaps.

### Pass 2 — Decisive Evidence

Examine primary, official, scientific, or original-data sources for important claims.

### Pass 3 — Independent and Contrary Evidence

Search for independent evidence, objections, inconsistent findings, and alternative explanations.

### Pass 4 — Gap and Conflict Resolution

Run targeted searches for unresolved numbers, terminology, versions, or contradictions.

### Pass 5 — Sufficiency Assessment

Determine whether another source is likely to change the conclusion or merely add repetitive information.

Do not execute every pass merely to preserve the template. Stop earlier when the question reaches sufficient coverage.

## Search-Bias Control

- Do not rely only on top-ranked search-engine results.
- Do not use the user's wording as the only query formulation.
- Do not build the conclusion only from supporting sources.
- Do not substitute a headline or abstract for the necessary underlying text.
- Do not count multiple republications of one origin as independent evidence.
- Include language, regional, and access limitations in the reported limitations.

## Presentable Search Record

Record the types of searches, source categories examined, and remaining gaps at a summary level. Provide raw search queries or internal notes only when explicitly requested.
