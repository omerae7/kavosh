# Research Depth and Evidence Rigor

## Purpose

Set research depth separately from evidence rigor. Depth controls the amount and complexity of research work. Rigor controls the strength of checks needed to reduce the consequences of error.

## Initial Assessment

Evaluate:

- the main question and expected output;
- the number of independent subquestions;
- information dispersion or access difficulty;
- topic freshness and rate of change;
- likelihood of source conflict;
- the need for scientific, primary, independent, or real-world evidence;
- decision importance and cost of error;
- possible harm from an incorrect answer;
- quality of the available data;
- and dependence on the user's specific circumstances.

If the answer depends on an important unknown user characteristic, ask a follow-up question only when that uncertainty would materially change the result. Otherwise report the limitation or provide qualitative cases.

## Research Depth

### Quick

Select for a bounded question with a clear answer and a small number of decisive sources. Search purposefully, inspect the suitable primary source, and skip low-value side paths.

### Standard

Select for a multi-faceted question, comparison, claim requiring confirmation, or topic requiring several source types. Create subquestions, examine more than one relevant perspective, and perform proportionate contradiction and freshness checks.

### Deep

Select for a complex, multi-stage, dispersed, disputed, or high-impact problem. Run multiple search passes, trace primary sources, examine contrary evidence, activate specialist analysis, and review remaining gaps.

Do not increase depth merely because the user says “complete research.” Also consider scope, expected marginal value, and tool or time limits. When the user explicitly requests greater depth, follow that request as far as possible and disclose any consequential limitation.

## Evidence Rigor

### Normal

Use for low-risk topics with straightforward, readily verifiable claims. Check claim-to-source support, necessary freshness, and the absence of obvious contradiction.

### Enhanced

Use for an important purchase, business decision, consequential comparison, disputed claim, or information involving commercial interests. Combine primary and independent sources when available and audit decisive claims individually.

### Sensitive

Use for medical, health, legal, high-risk financial, safety, or otherwise high-consequence topics. Apply stronger checks to current official and primary sources, authoritative guidance, independent evidence, limitations, applicability, and red-team review. Do not present the result as a substitute for personalized professional advice.

Do not equate Sensitive rigor with Deep research. A narrow question may require Quick depth with Sensitive rigor.

## Module Selection

- **Quick depth:** Use the minimum necessary modules and direct claim controls.
- **Standard depth:** Combine source types and include at least one gap or contradiction review.
- **Deep depth:** Use multiple search passes, the necessary specialist modules, and the Red Team.
- **Enhanced rigor:** Activate the Source and Claim Auditor for decisive findings.
- **Sensitive rigor:** Activate the Source and Claim Auditor, freshness control, and Red Team; make uncertainty explicit.

## Reconfiguration Criteria

Revise depth or rigor during the investigation when:

- a material conflict between sources appears;
- an initial claim leads to a more sensitive issue;
- a decisive source is unavailable;
- the result becomes sensitive to an unknown variable;
- or new evidence changes the main question.

Record the change in the Kavosh Execution Trace without exposing private reasoning details.
